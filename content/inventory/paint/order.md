Great collection already — you’ve got a solid primary range **plus** a bunch of desaturated, flesh, earth, and fantasy hues through Army Painter.
Given everything you own, the best additions are those that **expand what you *cannot* currently do easily** — especially for **airbrushing, glazing, and high-impact effects**, where acrylic inks shine far more than miniature paints.

Below are curated *top recommendations*, grouped by what they bring to your workflow.

---

# ⭐ **Top 4 Recommendations (High Impact / Most Versatile)**

## **1. Turquoise (Liquitex Acrylic Ink)**

### **Why you want this**

* You already have **Phthalo Blue GS**, but Turquoise sits in that magical zone between green and blue.
* Airbrushes beautifully for:

  * Energy effects
  * Magical glows
  * Plasma / OSL
  * Dragon scales / sci-fi armor
* Mixes well with **Quinacridone Magenta** for gorgeous violets and cold shadows.

If you only buy one, make it this.

---

## **2. Prism Violet *or* Dioxazine Purple**

### **Why you want this**

You have some purples in Army Painter, but **inks give depth, transparency, and saturation you can’t get from paint**.

* Great for:

  * Rich shadows
  * Glazes
  * Blending into blues/magentas
  * Ethereal or arcane effects
* **Prism Violet** = cleaner, lighter, more vibrant
* **Dioxazine Purple** = deeper, more dramatic, amazing for shadows

**If you airbrush a lot → get Dioxazine Purple.
If you glaze and highlight → Prism Violet.**

---

## **3. Transparent Raw Sienna *or* Sepia**

### **Why you want this**

Your set lacks a **warm glazing ink** for:

* Weathering
* Bone tones
* Metallic shading
* Leather
* Aged parchment
* Warm shadows on skin tones

**Transparent Raw Sienna** = perfect golden warmth
**Sepia** = more brown, more vintage/aged look

You already own Transparent Burnt Umber → a Raw Sienna or Sepia fills the *warm midtone* gap.

---

## **4. Fluorescent Pink *or* Fluorescent Green**

### **Why you want this**

You don’t have *any* true fluorescents yet, and inks make them actually usable.

Great for:

* Magical glow effects
* Cosmic/nebula patterns
* Energy coils or plasma guns
* Bright OSL
* Airbrushed gradients

**Fluorescent Pink** is incredibly flexible when mixed with magenta/purple.
**Fluorescent Green** is the best for sci-fi, undead, and toxic effects.

If you do fantasy or sci-fi → this is a huge upgrade.

---

# 🧪 **Secondary Strong Picks (More Niche but Excellent)**

## **5. Perylene Maroon**

* Deep, rich, moody red-black
* Amazing for shading red armor, skin, leather
* Perfect for blood shadows or gothic palettes

## **6. Vivid Lime Green**

* A cleaner, non-fluorescent bright green
* Ideal for bright foliage or magical green glows
* Fills a gap between jungle greens and neon greens

## **7. Naphthol Crimson or Pyrrole Red**

Your red range is okay but:

* Army Painter reds are desaturated compared to inks
* **Naphthol Crimson** = clean warm red, great mixing base
* **Pyrrole Red** = deeper, fiery, powerful

If you struggle getting *vibrant* reds → pick one.

## **8. Turquoise Deep**

* A darker turquoise, excellent for shadows
* Blends beautifully with standard Turquoise

Only get this *if* you pick the regular turquoise too.

---

# ❌ Items you *don’t* need more of

You already own:

* Titanium White (ink)
* Carbon Black (ink)
* Phthalo Blue GS
* Transparent Burnt Umber
* Quinacridone Magenta
* Yellow Medium Azo

So skip duplicates unless you’re running low.
