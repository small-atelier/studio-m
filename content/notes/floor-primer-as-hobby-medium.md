{{< lead >}}
Leftover acrylic floor primer (Torrbruksprodukter *Primer Golv*) covers most of what Mod Podge and PVA do for terrain and basing, and pairs with leftover flytspackel for a hard shell on XPS foam.
{{< /lead >}}

## What it is

*Primer Golv* is a **styrene-acrylic dispersion**: water 45–55 %, styrene-acrylic polymer 45–55 %, 2-amino-2-methylpropanol < 1.5 % (pH buffer). It's the same family of binder as acrylic mediums and most basing glues. Water clean-up while wet, waterproof once cured. Store frost-free.

Dries to a thin, slightly flexible film, like a coat of acrylic varnish. It only feels rubbery when pooled thick on a non-porous surface and peeled off.

## Condition check

| Good | Bad |
|---|---|
| Milky white, smooth like thin cream after shaking | Lumpy, curdled, or a separated layer that won't mix back (it has been frozen) |
| Dries clear to satin in 30–60 min on scrap | Sour or rotten smell |
| Dry film peels as one flexible piece | A thick skin across the top, or a dry film that crumbles |

## Versus Mod Podge / PVA

| Use | Floor primer | Mod Podge / PVA |
|---|---|---|
| Sealing foam, MDF, cardboard, plaster | ✅ | ✅ |
| Locking sand, gravel or ballast (diluted drip) | ✅ | ✅ |
| Texture paste with grit | ✅ | ✅ |
| Static grass, flock | ⚠️ skins fast, less tack | ✅ PVA better |
| Gluing paper or labels (decoupage) | ⚠️ slides around | ✅ |
| Finish | one sheen, satin to gloss | matte / satin / gloss variants |
| Plastic to plastic | ❌ use plastic cement or CA | ❌ |

## Dilutions

| Job | Primer : water |
|---|---|
| Sealing XPS foam | neat or barely diluted, 2 coats |
| Sealing MDF, cardboard, plaster | 1 : 1 |
| Fixing sand or gravel after gluing | 1 : 3, dripped on so it soaks in |
| Texture paste | neat, with sand or grit stirred in |

## Mixing with acrylic paint

It mixes with acrylic paint like any acrylic medium:

- **Tinted sealer.** Add a splash of cheap craft acrylic to seal and base-coat terrain in one pass.
- **Coloured texture paste.** Primer, sand and paint together give pre-coloured groundwork.
- **Tougher craft paint.** A bit mixed into craft acrylics makes them more durable and better at sticking.

More primer means less opacity and more sheen. Use it with craft paint, not mini paints. Mix a small test cup first, because the odd pairing of acrylic dispersions curdles.

### Leftover exterior paint

Leftover water-based exterior acrylic (*akrylatfärg*, e.g. trim or cladding paint) beats craft paint here: heavier pigment, and made to stand weather. *Skall ej spädas* on the can is about hiding power on its intended job, not compatibility. These paints contain isothiazolinone preservatives, which are skin sensitisers, so wear gloves.

Two premixes cover most jobs:

| Bottle | Paint : primer | For |
|---|---|---|
| **SEALER** | 2 : 1 | Base coat and foam sealing in one pass |
| **BINDER** | 1 : 2 | Liquid base for sludge and texture paste |

- The paint alone already seals XPS. The primer in SEALER stretches it and makes the film more flexible.
- Add grit and powder to BINDER in a cup per session, never in the bottle.
- Flytspackel and gypsum lighten BINDER to dark grey. A splash of SEALER in the cup brings it back to black.
- Semi-matt paint with satin primer dries shinier than either one alone.

## Storing mixes

Empty 1 l resin bottles (opaque HDPE, tight cap) suit the liquid premixes. The narrow neck rules out anything with grit or setting powder.

**Cleaning:**

1. Drain upside down, then wipe the neck and cap thread.
2. Rinse with used IPA. Collect it in a waste bottle labelled "IPA + resin waste, flammable", not filled to the top, and hand it in as *farligt avfall*.
3. Rinse with fresh IPA, including the cap and its seal. Pour this rinse into the used-IPA bottle afterwards.
4. Air out with the cap off until there's no IPA smell, then rinse with water.

Wear gloves throughout, because resin dissolved in IPA gets through skin more easily.

**Labelling and filling:**

- Peel off the resin label so the bottle doesn't read as resin next to the printer.
- On both bottle and cap, write SEALER or BINDER, the ratio, the mix date and **NOT RESIN**. Use permanent marker or tape over the label so drips don't smudge it.
- Fill to 80–90 % and squeeze the air out before capping to slow skinning. Add 2–3 stainless nuts as shaker balls, because plain steel rusts.
- Store frost-free.

## On XPS foam

- **Safe.** It's water-based with no solvents, so it won't melt the foam.
- **Forms a skin.** XPS is closed-cell, so the primer sits on top instead of soaking in.
- **Scuff smooth factory faces first** with sandpaper or a ball of foil, or the primer beads up.
- **Before spray primer,** check that every pinhole and carved crack is sealed, because any gap lets the spray's solvent eat the foam underneath. Test on a scrap: seal it, dry it overnight, then spray it.

## Hard shells: flytspackel, gypsum, filler

The primer alone protects against solvent, not knocks. For a hard shell, mix in a powder:

| | Flytspackel | Gypsum | Fix spackle (bag) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Base | cement | gypsum | usually gypsum |
| Hardness | hardest | hard | softer |
| Working time | short | 10–20 min | longer |
| Best for | tough outer shell on handled terrain | cheap hard shell, takes drybrushing well | carving or scribing detail after it sets (cracks, stonework, tracks) |

**Mixing:**

1. Mix the liquid first: about 1 part primer to 1–2 parts water.
2. Add powder to the liquid, never the other way round, until it's like yoghurt for brushing or thicker for texture.
3. Mix flytspackel much thicker than the bag says. At the bag's ratio it's made to flow flat across a floor and runs off foam.

**Applying:**

- Mix in small cups, because anything left over sets in the cup.
- Brush or dab on thin coats. It's a shell over the foam, not a cast, so thick layers add weight and crack off when the foam flexes.
- Flytspackel is alkaline: wear gloves, and wear a dust mask when tipping out the dry powder.
- If an opened bag has drawn moisture, the powder will have hard lumps. Sieve them out or bin the bag.
