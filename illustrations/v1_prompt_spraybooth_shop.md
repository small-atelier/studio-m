- A plywood cabinet on short legs, wide enough for two separate working bays side by side.
- A removable plexiglass panel standing vertically between the two bays, dividing them (drawn slightly pulled up/out of its slot to show it lifts out).
- A plexiglass panel across the entire top of the cabinet, with a thin LED light strip built into the underside of the roof structure along the front edge, shown with a few short light rays.
- At the back of the cabinet, draw a cutaway revealing an internal plenum where the two bays' extraction openings merge, containing a baffle shaped like a wide letter S that the airflow must pass through — draw dashed arrows following the air path bending through the S-shape before reaching a fan.
- From the fan, a short flexible ribbed hose runs to a round duct fitting fixed in a wall behind the booth.

Beside the cabinet, on the floor:
- One upright shop air compressor with a visible round tank.
- A single air line leaving the compressor into an inline moisture trap (small canister with a drain valve).

At each bay, on the work surface, draw a small PET plastic bottle with a cyclone-style lid (swirl-shaped inlet) sitting off to the side of the airbrush holder — a cleaning/rinse waste catcher, clearly its own separate object, not connected by any tube or hose to the extraction duct or fan.
